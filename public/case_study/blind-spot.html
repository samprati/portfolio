Beat 4 · The restraint decision

The system chooses the safety net. The owner sees one thing.

With the economics fixed, the temptation was to hand the owner every safety net — check-ins, on-demand, scheduled visits — and call it “powerful.” That's not power. It's decision fatigue and cost. So I bound each safety net to unit state and let the system choose. Flip the state — same card, one clear action:

Restraint was the design. All three ideas are in the product — but the owner never manages a menu of them. That's the difference between “more features” and a system.

Beat 7 · Operate the system

One component. Three roles. Three truths.

A product this multi-sided is a design-system achievement, not a styling one. A single “Property Status Card” renders financial yield to the owner, but only maintenance status to the tenant, and a checklist to the agent — the logic lives in the component, not in three forked front-ends. Beat 8 · Beyond the happy path & validation

The scenarios real life throws — and what I'd test.

  • The agent with no signal: inspections happen in basements and unoccupied towers. Capture caches offline and syncs when it can — it can never depend on a good connection.
  • The disputed repair: when owner and tenant disagree on a cost, the geotagged photo trail is the neutral record. The product takes no side — it shows the evidence.
  • Tenant privacy: “eyes on the ground” must never feel like surveillance of an occupied home. Inspections of tenanted units are scheduled, consented, and scoped — the owner sees the asset's condition, not the tenant's life.

Three hypotheses that carry the whole architecture

  • Unit economics: can an agent complete a standard inspection in ≤ ~15 min — the threshold that keeps the model profitable?
  • Visual reassurance: do remote owners report a measurable anxiety drop from the image feed vs. an emailed PDF report?
  • B2B scalability: can the dashboard surface one neglected unit inside a 50+ portfolio without overwhelming the user?
The most useful thing I did was refuse the obvious solution — and be willing to kill my own favourite idea when the economics didn't hold. The honest limit is equally clear: without primary research this is a well-reasoned hypothesis, not a validated product. Knowing which is which is the job.

I built this to be scrutinised. Ask me why I rejected an option, or push on a trade-off I accepted — those are the conversations this is designed to start.
